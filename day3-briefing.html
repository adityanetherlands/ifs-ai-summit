Working Group Output

What each group needs to leave with

By playback, each group should be able to say what changes, what gets measured, what must be decided, and who owns the first proof point.

The internal pace of change must match or beat the market.

The working groups should be honest about where IFS is preserving old operating assumptions. The question is not whether AI changes the function. It is whether we choose the change deliberately and early enough.

Key AI-native KPIs

Define the few measurements that would prove the function is becoming faster, higher quality, more automated, or more valuable.

Commitments

Name the specific commitments the group is willing to stand behind after the summit.

Follow-up actions

Capture the next steps, owners, and timing needed to keep momentum after the trip.

Fork-in-the-road decisions

Identify the decisions that cannot be deferred: operating model, product model, talent model, process ownership, or investment choices.

First proof point

Define a visible 30 to 90 day proof point that shows the function is behaving differently, not just talking differently.

What stops or changes

Be explicit about which habits, handoffs, approval loops, or manual work should not survive the AI-native plan.

Room Rules

How to use the day well

Day 3 works if the conversation is candid, quantified, and decision-oriented.

Bring yesterday into the room

Use what you experienced on Day 2 as evidence. The plan should be grounded in what now feels possible.

  • What moved faster than expected?
  • What quality level surprised you?

Quantify the ambition

If the target is not uncomfortable, it probably is not taking the market seriously enough.

  • Set aggressive functional targets.
  • Pair every target with a proof point.

Name the tradeoffs

The best working groups will surface the choices leadership needs to make, not smooth them over.

  • What must be funded?
  • What must be stopped?
